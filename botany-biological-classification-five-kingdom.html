Lesson 2 • Biological Classification

2️⃣ Five Kingdom Classification

R. H. Whittaker (1969) proposed the Five Kingdom Classification system to classify living organisms more naturally using several important biological criteria.

🏆 The Five Kingdoms

1. Monera: Prokaryotic organisms such as bacteria and cyanobacteria.
2. Protista: Mainly unicellular eukaryotic organisms.
3. Fungi: Heterotrophic eukaryotes with absorptive nutrition.
4. Plantae: Multicellular autotrophic organisms with chlorophyll.
5. Animalia: Multicellular heterotrophic organisms with ingestive nutrition.

🔬 Main Criteria Used by Whittaker

Cell structure: Prokaryotic or eukaryotic organisation.
Body organisation: Unicellular or multicellular nature and level of organisation.
Mode of nutrition: Autotrophic or heterotrophic; ingestion or absorption.
Reproduction: Important reproductive characteristics.
Phylogenetic relationships: Evolutionary relationships among organisms.

🧠 NEET Key Points

✓ Five Kingdom Classification was proposed by R. H. Whittaker in 1969.
✓ The kingdoms are Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
✓ Cell structure and mode of nutrition are major classification criteria.
✓ The system considers evolutionary relationships.
