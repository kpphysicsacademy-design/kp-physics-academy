Gymnosperms

Naked-seeded plants: cones, heterospory, pollination and key NCERT adaptations.

🎯 Learning Objective

Understand why gymnosperms are called naked-seeded plants and explain their cones, spores and reproductive cycle.

📖 Simple Explanation

In gymnosperms, ovules are not enclosed by an ovary wall. After fertilisation, the seeds remain exposed—therefore they are called naked-seeded plants. They are typically trees or shrubs.

🔑 Key Characteristics

  • Well-developed sporophytic plant body with roots, stems and leaves.
  • Heterosporous: produce microspores and megaspores.
  • Sporophylls form male and female cones (strobili).
  • Examples: Cycas, Pinus, Cedrus.

🌱 Special Adaptations

  • Pinus: mycorrhizal roots.
  • Cycas: coralloid roots associated with nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.
  • Conifers often have needle-like leaves, thick cuticle and sunken stomata that reduce water loss.

🧬 Reproduction: Step by Step

MICROSPOROPHYLL → POLLEN GRAIN → WIND POLLINATION → POLLEN TUBE → ARCHEGONIUM → FERTILISATION → EMBRYO → NAKED SEED
  1. Microsporangia in male cones produce microspores that develop into pollen grains.
  2. Female cones bear megasporophylls carrying ovules.
  3. One megaspore develops into the female gametophyte within the ovule.
  4. Pollen reaches the ovule and a pollen tube carries male gametes towards the archegonia.
  5. After fertilisation, the zygote forms an embryo and the ovule develops into an exposed seed.

🧩 Male Cone vs Female Cone

♂ Male cone

Microsporophylls bear microsporangia that form microspores. The reduced male gametophyte is the pollen grain.

♀ Female cone

Megasporophylls bear ovules/megaspore-containing structures. The female gametophyte develops within the ovule.

⭐ Important NCERT Examples

  • Cycas: unbranched stem and coralloid roots associated with nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.
  • Pinus: branched stem, mycorrhizal roots and needle-like leaves.
  • Cedrus: branched gymnosperm tree.
  • Sequoia: among the tallest tree species.

💡 Example-Based Thinking

Question: A plant has exposed ovules, produces pollen and megaspores, and forms cones but no fruits. Which group does it belong to?

Answer: Gymnosperms — because their ovules and seeds are not enclosed within an ovary or fruit.

⚠️ NEET Traps

  • Naked seeds ≠ no seeds; the seeds are present but not enclosed in fruits.
  • Gymnosperms are heterosporous.
  • Cycas has coralloid roots; Pinus has mycorrhiza.
  • Needle leaves + thick cuticle + sunken stomata help reduce water loss.

📝 Quick Recap

Naked ovules → heterospory → cones → pollen → fertilisation → naked seeds.
