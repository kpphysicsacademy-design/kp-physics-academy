Reproduction & Seed Habit

See how plant reproduction evolves from spores to seeds and why heterospory is a key step toward the seed habit.

🎯 Learning Objective

Explain spore reproduction, homospory and heterospory, identify the origin of seed habit, and distinguish gymnosperm and angiosperm reproduction for NEET.

🧬 Reproductive Evolution Flow

1. SporesAlgae, bryophytes and pteridophytes reproduce through spores.
2. Homospory → HeterosporyTwo types of spores create separate male and female gametophytes.
3. Megaspore retentionThe female gametophyte develops within the megaspore, improving protection.
4. Seed habitThe embryo gains protection and nourishment inside a seed.

🔍 Learn One Difference at a Time

Spore vs Seed

A spore is usually a single reproductive cell that can develop into a new individual. A seed contains an embryo with stored nourishment and a protective covering.

Homospory vs Heterospory

Homospory produces one kind of spore. Heterospory produces microspores and megaspores; in pteridophytes, Selaginella and Salvinia are classic NCERT examples.

Gymnosperm vs Angiosperm seed

Gymnosperm ovules and seeds are not enclosed in an ovary. In angiosperms, ovules are enclosed within the ovary and seeds later occur within fruits.

⚠️ NEET Quick Rules

  • Motile male gametes and external water requirement are important in bryophytes and pteridophytes.
  • Heterospory is an important evolutionary precursor to seed habit.
  • Selaginella and Salvinia are heterosporous pteridophytes.
  • Double fertilisation is characteristic of angiosperms.
  • Gymnosperms have naked seeds.
