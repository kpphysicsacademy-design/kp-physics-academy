Concept 9: Reproduction, Spores, Seeds & Fertilisation

Understand how reproductive structures change from simpler plant groups to seed plants.

  • Spores are common reproductive units in cryptogams.
  • Water is essential for fertilisation in bryophytes and pteridophytes.
  • Heterospory is an important evolutionary step toward seed habit.
  • Gymnosperms produce seeds but do not form fruits.
  • Angiosperms show double fertilisation and produce seeds enclosed within fruits.

NEET Evolution Flow

  • Spore formation: common in algae, bryophytes and pteridophytes.
  • Homospory: one type of spore generally produces similar gametophytes.
  • Heterospory: microspores and megaspores produce separate male and female gametophytes.
  • Seed habit: develops through retention of the megaspore and protection of the developing embryo.
  • Gymnosperms: ovules and seeds are not enclosed in an ovary.
  • Angiosperms: pollen reaches the stigma; double fertilisation produces zygote and endosperm.

High-Yield NEET Points

  • Water is required for fertilisation in bryophytes and pteridophytes because male gametes are motile.
  • Heterospory is seen in Selaginella and Salvinia among pteridophytes.
  • Double fertilisation is unique to angiosperms.
