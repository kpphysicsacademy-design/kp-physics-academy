🧠 Topics & Detailed Concepts

1. What is Living?

Explains the characteristics that distinguish living organisms from non-living things.

  • Growth: Increase in size and mass through internal cellular processes.
  • Reproduction: Production of offspring.
  • Metabolism: All chemical reactions occurring within a living organism.
  • Cellular Organisation: Organised structure based on cells.
  • Consciousness: Ability to sense and respond to environmental stimuli.

2. Biodiversity

Refers to the enormous variety of living organisms present on Earth.

  • Diversity in the Living World: Organisms differ in size, shape, structure, habitat and lifestyle.
  • Need for Classification: Classification helps organise and study the huge diversity of organisms systematically.

3. Taxonomy and Systematics

  • Identification: Recognising an organism and determining what it is.
  • Classification: Arranging organisms into groups based on similarities and differences.
  • Taxonomy: Study of identification, nomenclature and classification.
  • Systematics: Study of diversity and relationships among organisms, including evolutionary relationships.

4. Nomenclature

  • Scientific Names: Universal names used to identify organisms accurately.
  • Binomial Nomenclature: Each organism has a two-word scientific name: Genus + species.
  • Rules of Nomenclature: Scientific names follow internationally accepted conventions.

5. Taxonomic Categories

Hierarchical levels used to classify organisms.

Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum/Division → Kingdom

  • Species: basic unit of classification.
  • Genus: group of closely related species.
  • Family: group of related genera.
  • Order: group of related families.
  • Class: group of related orders.
  • Phylum/Division: group of related classes; phylum is generally used for animals and division for plants.
  • Kingdom: a major taxonomic category containing related phyla or divisions.

6. Taxonomical Aids

  • Herbarium: Collection of preserved, dried and labelled plant specimens.
  • Botanical Gardens: Places where living plants are cultivated and maintained for study.
  • Museum: Collection of preserved plant and animal specimens.
  • Zoological Parks: Places where living wild animals are kept and studied.
  • Key: Tool used to identify organisms using contrasting characteristics.
  • Flora: Information about plants found in a particular geographical area.
  • Manuals: Books containing information for identifying organisms.
  • Monographs: Detailed scientific accounts of a particular taxonomic group.
  • Catalogues: Lists containing information about organisms and specimens.
