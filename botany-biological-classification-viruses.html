Lesson 6 • Biological Classification

🦠 Viruses

Main concept: Viruses are acellular infectious agents containing either DNA or RNA enclosed in a protein coat. They can multiply only inside suitable living host cells.

📚 Learn Viruses in Detail

🧬 1. Acellular Nature
Viruses are not made of cells. They do not have cytoplasm, cell organelles or an independent cellular metabolism like living organisms.
🧪 2. Genetic Material
A virus contains either DNA or RNA as its genetic material. A single virus particle does not contain both types as its genome.
🛡️ 3. Protein Coat
The viral genetic material is enclosed within a protective protein coat called a capsid. The capsid is made of protein subunits called capsomeres.
🏠 4. Obligate Intracellular Parasites
Viruses multiply only inside living host cells. They use the host cell's machinery to produce viral components and assemble new virus particles.
⚖️ 5. Living and Non-living Characteristics
Outside a host cell, viruses are metabolically inactive and may even be crystallised. Inside a suitable host cell, they reproduce and show characteristics associated with living systems.
🌱 6. Host Range
Different viruses infect different hosts. Viruses may infect bacteria, plants, animals and other organisms. A virus that infects bacteria is called a bacteriophage.
🔬 7. Discovery and Examples
Viruses are associated with diseases in plants and animals. Tobacco mosaic virus (TMV) is an important example in the history of plant virology.

🧠 NEET Focus

✓ Viruses are acellular infectious agents.
✓ Viral genome contains either DNA or RNA.
✓ The protein coat is called a capsid.
✓ Viruses multiply only inside living host cells.
✓ Bacteriophages infect bacteria.
✓ Outside the host, viruses are metabolically inactive.
