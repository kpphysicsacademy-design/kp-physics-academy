🌿 CONCEPT 4 • PLANT KINGDOM

Pteridophytes

Seedless vascular plants: ferns, horsetails, spores, prothallus and heterospory.

🎯 Learning Objective

Identify why pteridophytes are called the first terrestrial vascular plants and understand their sporophyte-dominant life cycle.

📖 Core idea

Pteridophytes include ferns and horsetails. They possess xylem and phloem, but unlike gymnosperms and angiosperms they do not form seeds. Their main plant body is a dominant sporophyte with true roots, stems and leaves.

VascularSeedlessSporesSporophyte dominant

🌱 Plant body & reproduction

  • Sporophyte bears sporangia, often associated with sporophylls.
  • Spores form a small, free-living, usually photosynthetic gametophyte called prothallus.
  • Antheridia and archegonia develop on the gametophyte.
  • Water is required for fertilisation because male gametes must reach the archegonium.
SPOROPHYTE → SPORANGIA → MEIOSIS → SPORES → PROTHALLUS → FERTILISATION → ZYGOTE → SPOROPHYTE

🔬 Homospory vs Heterospory

Homosporous

One kind of spore; common condition in many pteridophytes.

Heterosporous

Microspores and megaspores; examples include Selaginella and Salvinia.

Heterospory and retention of female gametophytes are important evolutionary steps towards the seed habit.

⭐ Examples

Selaginella, Lycopodium, Equisetum, Dryopteris, Pteris, Adiantum.

⚠️ NEET traps

  • They are vascular but seedless.
  • Dominant generation is the sporophyte, unlike bryophytes.
  • Prothallus is the gametophyte.
  • Selaginella and Salvinia are heterosporous.

📝 Quick Recap

True roots + stems + leaves → vascular tissue → spores → prothallus → water for fertilisation.
