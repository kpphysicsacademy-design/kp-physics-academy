Biological Classification • Concept Lesson

Kingdom Protista

Main concept: Protists are primarily unicellular eukaryotic organisms. Important groups include chrysophytes, dinoflagellates, euglenoids, slime moulds and protozoans.

📚 Learn in Detail

What are Protists?
Kingdom Protista includes mainly unicellular eukaryotic organisms. They possess a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
1. Chrysophytes
This group includes diatoms and golden algae (desmids). They are generally found in freshwater and marine environments. Diatom cell walls contain silica.
2. Dinoflagellates
Most are marine and photosynthetic. Many have cellulose plates on the cell surface and two flagella. Some species can cause red tides.
3. Euglenoids
Commonly found in stagnant freshwater. They lack a cell wall but possess a protein-rich pellicle. They may show both autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition.
4. Slime Moulds
They are saprophytic protists. During favourable conditions they form a plasmodial mass, while unfavourable conditions lead to resistant spores.
5. Protozoans
Protozoans are heterotrophic protists. Major groups include amoeboid, flagellated, ciliated and sporozoan forms.

🧠 NEET Focus

✓ Protists are eukaryotic and mainly unicellular.
✓ Diatoms have siliceous cell walls.
✓ Euglenoids possess a pellicle instead of a cell wall.
✓ Dinoflagellates may produce red tides.
✓ Protozoans are heterotrophic protists.
