Lesson 7 • Biological Classification

🧬 Viroids

Main concept: Viroids are very small infectious agents made only of naked RNA. Unlike viruses, they do not have a protein coat.

📚 Learn Viroids in Detail

🧪 1. What are Viroids?
Viroids are infectious agents composed of small RNA molecules. They are simpler in structure than viruses because they lack a protein coat.
🧬 2. Naked RNA
The viroid molecule consists of naked RNA. This RNA is responsible for infection and replication in a suitable host.
🚫 3. No Capsid
A major difference between viruses and viroids is that viruses have a protein coat called a capsid, whereas viroids do not possess a capsid.
🌱 4. Plant Diseases
Viroids are important plant pathogens and can cause diseases in crop plants. They demonstrate that even a very small RNA molecule can act as an infectious agent.
🔬 5. Discovery
Viroids were discovered by Theodor O. Diener. Their discovery expanded scientific understanding of infectious agents.
⚖️ 6. Viroid vs Virus
Viroid: naked RNA and no protein coat.
Virus: genetic material enclosed in a protein coat and may have additional structural components.
