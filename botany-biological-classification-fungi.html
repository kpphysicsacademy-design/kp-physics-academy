Lesson 5 • Biological Classification

🍄 Kingdom Fungi

Main concept: Fungi are eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms that generally have chitinous cell walls. Most fungi absorb nutrients from organic matter, and their body commonly consists of hyphae forming a mycelium.

📚 Learn Fungi in Detail

🍄 1. General Characteristics
Fungi are eukaryotic and mostly multicellular, although yeasts are unicellular. They are heterotrophs and obtain nutrients by absorption.
🧱 2. Cell Wall
The fungal cell wall is mainly composed of chitin and polysaccharides. Fungi do not possess chlorophyll and therefore cannot perform photosynthesis.
🌿 3. Body Organisation
The body of most fungi consists of long, thread-like structures called hyphae. A network of hyphae forms the mycelium.
🍽️ 4. Mode of Nutrition
Fungi may be saprophytic, parasitic or symbiotic. Saprophytic fungi feed on dead organic matter, while parasitic fungi obtain food from living hosts.
🔁 5. Reproduction
Fungi reproduce vegetatively, asexually and sexually. Asexual reproduction commonly occurs through spores such as conidia or sporangiospores, depending on the group.
🧬 6. Major Groups
In traditional NCERT classification, important fungal groups include Phycomycetes, Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes and Deuteromycetes. Learn their characteristic spores, examples and reproductive features.
🤝 7. Symbiotic Associations
Fungi form important associations such as lichens with algae and mycorrhiza with plant roots. These relationships can benefit both partners.

🧠 NEET Focus

✓ Fungi are eukaryotic heterotrophs.
✓ Their cell walls generally contain chitin.
✓ Hyphae collectively form a mycelium.
✓ Nutrition is commonly absorptive.
✓ Major NCERT groups include Phycomycetes, Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes and Deuteromycetes.
