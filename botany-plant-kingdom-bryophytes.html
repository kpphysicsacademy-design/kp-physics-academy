Bryophytes

Mosses and liverworts: habitat, structure, reproduction, life cycle and NEET-important differences.

🎯 Learning Objective

Understand why bryophytes are called amphibians of the plant kingdom and distinguish liverworts from mosses using NCERT features.

📖 Core idea

Bryophytes commonly occur in moist, humid and shaded habitats. They can live on soil but depend on water for sexual reproduction, so they are called amphibians of the plant kingdom.

MossesLiverwortsWater needed for fertilisationGametophyte dominant

🔑 Plant body

  • More differentiated than algae; may be thalloid, prostrate or erect.
  • Attached to the substratum by rhizoids.
  • They lack true roots, stems and leaves, though root-like, stem-like or leaf-like structures may occur.
  • The main plant body is haploid and gamete-producing: the gametophyte.

🧬 Reproduction & life cycle

GAMETOPHYTE → ANTHERIDIUM + ARCHEGONIUM → FERTILISATION IN WATER → ZYGOTE → DEPENDENT SPOROPHYTE → MEIOSIS → SPORES → GAMETOPHYTE
  • Antheridium: male sex organ; produces antherozoids.
  • Archegonium: flask-shaped female sex organ; produces one egg.
  • The sporophyte remains attached to and receives nourishment from the photosynthetic gametophyte.

🌱 Liverworts vs Mosses

🍀 Liverworts

Usually grow in moist, shady habitats. The body may be thalloid and dorsiventral, as in Marchantia. Asexual reproduction may occur through gemmae in gemma cups.

🌿 Mosses

Examples include Funaria, Polytrichum and Sphagnum. The life cycle includes an early protonema stage followed by the leafy gametophytic stage.

🌍 Ecological & economic importance

  • Important pioneers in plant succession on bare rocks and soil.
  • Moss mats can reduce the impact of rain and help prevent soil erosion.
  • Sphagnum can form peat and has high water-holding capacity.

⚠️ NEET traps

  • Bryophytes are not truly vascular plants.
  • Water dependence is especially important for sexual reproduction.
  • The dominant plant body is the gametophyte, while the sporophyte is dependent.
  • Do not confuse rhizoids with true roots.

📝 Quick Recap

Moist habitat → rhizoids → gametophyte dominant → water for fertilisation → dependent sporophyte.
